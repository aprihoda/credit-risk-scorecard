Credit Risk Scorecard

Out-of-Time Validation Through the 2008 Crisis

Trained on 2000-2005 loans

Tested on 2006-2007 loans

Fannie Mae Single-Family Loan Performance Data

Anne M. Prihoda

What this report covers

• Builds a loan-level credit risk scorecard on 2000 to 2005 mortgage originations using predictor binning, Weight of Evidence, logistic regression, and points scaling

• Validates the scorecard out-of-time on 2006 to 2007 originations, which seasoned through the housing crisis

• Weight of Evidence (WOE) converts each predictor onto a common risk scale by comparing, within each range of values, the share of loans that repaid against the share that defaulted. Information Value (IV) sums that separation across all ranges into a single number measuring how much a predictor is worth

• Discrimination is measured by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic (KS), the widest gap between defaults captured and non-defaults captured, and by the ROC curve and the area beneath it (AUC)

• Calibration asks whether the predicted level of default is right, comparing predicted against actual by score band and by origination year. A model can rank loans correctly and still badly understate how many will default

• Population stability asks whether the applicants being scored still resemble those the model was built on, measured by the Population Stability Index (PSI), the standard early warning in model monitoring

• Data prepared by the ETL pipeline: 1,178,508,035 monthly records across 48 quarterly files, reconciled to Fannie Mae published statistical summaries

• 19.1 million prime, fully documented, fixed-rate mortgages acquired by Fannie Mae between 2000 and 2007

• Loan counts and default rates reconcile to Fannie Mae published summaries within a few basis points on every vintage

• Default rates hold between 1.2 and 1.6 percent for loans made from 2000 through 2003

• They then climb steeply: 3.2 percent in 2004, 6.0 percent in 2005, and 8 to 9 percent in 2006 and 2007

• That is a sevenfold deterioration inside the safest segment of the mortgage market

• It happened with no comparable movement in the characteristics lenders were underwriting on

• Discrimination asks whether the model puts riskier loans ahead of safer ones

• KS is the widest gap between defaults captured and non-defaults captured, working from worst score to best

• The ROC curve shows the same separation: The further it bows above the diagonal, the better the ranking

• The model was fitted on 2000 to 2005 loans and applied untouched to 2006 and 2007 loans it had never seen

• KS falls only from 37.3 to 32.1, and area under the curve from 0.746 to 0.716

• The ranking held: Riskier loans still scored worse and safer loans still scored better, on loans originated after the training period

• Ranking correctly is not the same as predicting correctly: Section 5 shows the predicted level of default was far too low

4. Model Discrimination

Out-of-time validation

Sample KS
Training 37.26%
Validation 32.05%

• Discrimination asks whether the ordering is right; Calibration asks whether the predicted level is right

• A model can rank every loan correctly and still be badly wrong about how many will default

• Ordering is flawless: Predicted default rises from 0.19 to 6.28 percent across deciles, actual from 0.54 to 19.64 percent

• Every decile is in the right place, and every decile is understated by a factor of three to four

• 2006 originations: Predicted default rate 2.10 percent, actual default rate 8.17 percent

• 2007 originations: Predicted default rate 2.26 percent, actual default rate 8.94 percent

• The model barely separated the two vintages because the applicants barely differed

• The difference was in the environment those loans matured into, which no origination characteristic can express

• PSI compares the score distribution of the new population against the population the model was built on

• Below 0.10 is stable and requires no action; 0.10 to 0.25 is a minor shift; Above 0.25 calls for investigation or a rebuild

• The index comes to 0.031, comfortably inside the stable range

• Applicants arriving in 2006 and 2007 looked very much like those the model was trained on

• A monitoring report built on this measure would have raised no alarm while the book defaulted at four times the predicted rate

• The scorecard method worked: It ranked risk correctly and its inputs stayed stable

• What failed was the assumption that the relationship between borrower characteristics and default holds steady over time

• Population monitoring cannot detect that failure because it watches applicants rather than outcomes

• Actual-versus-predicted tracking, shown in Section 5, is what would have caught it, within months of the 2006 vintage seasoning
